How to Write an Etsy Shop Description and About Section That Builds Trust

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Your shop description and About section are the parts of your store almost every serious buyer reads before a first purchase — and the parts most sellers leave half-finished or fill with 'Welcome to my shop! Thanks for stopping by!' A browsing shopper who's about to trust a stranger with their money is quietly asking three questions: who am I buying from, why should I trust them, and what makes this worth it? A good description answers all three. Here's how to write one.

Shop description vs. About section — two different jobs

  • The shop description is short, appears near your shop name, and is partly for search — it should say plainly what you sell and to whom, using words a buyer would actually search.
  • The About section is the longer story space: who you are, how you make things, and why you started. This is where trust is built and where a one-of-a-kind shop separates itself from a faceless dropshipper.
Buyers on handmade marketplaces are specifically looking for a human behind the product. A real story and a real face out-convert a polished-but-anonymous shop nearly every time. Your 'unprofessional' personal touch is the competitive advantage.

What to put in the shop description

Keep it to a few clear sentences that do three things:

  1. Say exactly what you sell, front-loading the phrase a buyer would search ('Hand-poured soy candles and wax melts in small-batch seasonal scents').
  2. Name who it's for or the occasion ('…perfect for cozy gifts and self-care').
  3. Add one trust signal — handmade to order, ships in 2 days, made in a smoke-free studio. One concrete detail beats three vague adjectives.

The About-section structure that works

Think of it as a short, honest story in four beats:

  1. The hook — one line on what you make and the feeling behind it.
  2. The origin — how you got started. Real beats impressive: a kitchen-table hobby that grew is more relatable than a corporate mission statement.
  3. The process — how items are made, the care and materials involved. This is where you justify a handmade price without ever mentioning price.
  4. The invitation — a warm close: custom orders welcome, message me with questions, thanks for supporting a small maker.

A quick template you can adapt

Hi, I'm [name] — I make [product] from [place/studio]. It started when [short, honest origin]. Every [item] is [made to order / small-batch / hand-finished] using [materials/quality note], because [what you care about]. If you'd like something customized, just send a message — I'd love to make it for you. Thank you for supporting a small shop.

Fill the brackets in your own voice, read it aloud, and cut anything that sounds like a press release. Warmth and specificity win; corporate polish loses.

Trust signals that quietly close the sale

  • Processing and shipping times stated plainly — uncertainty is a reason to leave.
  • A clear, calm policies note (returns, custom orders, care instructions) — see our return policy guide.
  • A real photo of you or your workspace — the fastest trust-builder on any handmade shop.
  • Consistency of voice across description, About, and listings — it reads as a real, cared-for business.

Frequently asked questions

What should an Etsy shop description say?

In a few clear sentences: exactly what you sell (front-loading a phrase buyers search), who it's for or the occasion, and one concrete trust signal like 'handmade to order' or 'ships in 2 days.' Keep it plain and searchable rather than clever — it partly feeds search and it's the first thing a browsing buyer reads.

What's the difference between the shop description and the About section?

The shop description is short, sits near your shop name, and helps with search — it states what you sell and to whom. The About section is the longer story space for who you are, how you make things, and why you started. The description informs; the About section builds the trust that converts a first-time buyer.

How do I write an About section that builds trust?

Tell a short, honest story in four beats: a hook about what you make, your real origin, your process and materials (which justifies a handmade price), and a warm invitation to reach out. Add a photo of you or your workspace and clear processing times. Real and specific beats polished and anonymous on a handmade marketplace.
