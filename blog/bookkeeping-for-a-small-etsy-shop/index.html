Bookkeeping for a Small Etsy Shop: A Simple System You'll Actually Keep Up

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read

"Bookkeeping" is one of those words that makes solo sellers want to close the tab. It sounds like ledgers, accountants, and things you're probably doing wrong. In reality, bookkeeping for a small shop is just writing down the money that comes in and goes out, sorted into a few consistent buckets. Do that, and you always know if you're profitable, you make better decisions, and tax season becomes copying totals instead of reconstructing a year from memory. Here's a system simple enough that you'll actually keep it up.

What bookkeeping actually is (and isn't)

Bookkeeping is recording transactions. That's it. It is not filing your taxes, and it is not something you need a CPA or $30/month software to begin. What you need is one place to capture two flows:

  • Money in — every sale, by date and amount, ideally with the platform's fees already visible.
  • Money out — every business expense: materials, fees, shipping, software, ads, supplies, and any equipment.
The number one bookkeeping mistake for small sellers isn't a math error — it's mixing personal and business money. Open a separate bank account or at least a dedicated card for the shop. When business money lives in its own place, bookkeeping becomes reading one statement instead of untangling which grocery run was actually packing tape.

The categories to use (and reuse forever)

Consistency is the whole game. Pick a fixed set of categories and use the exact same ones every month. These map cleanly onto what you'll need at tax time:

CategoryWhat goes here
RevenueGross sales before fees
COGS — materialsMaterials, packaging, direct production supplies
Platform & payment feesListing, transaction, processing fees
ShippingPostage and shipping supplies
Software & subscriptionsTools you run the shop with
AdvertisingEtsy Ads, promoted listings, photography
Other operatingBooth fees, home office, mileage, misc.

That's a working chart of accounts for a solo shop. The point isn't perfection — it's that "materials" means the same thing in January and November so your months are comparable and your Schedule C almost fills itself. (More on the tax side in our sales tax and 1099-K guide.)

Cash vs accrual, in one paragraph

You'll see these two words and they sound scary. They're not. Cash basis means you record money when it actually moves — a sale counts the day you're paid, an expense counts the day you pay it. Accrual records when it's earned or owed, regardless of when cash moves. For nearly every small seller, cash basis is simpler, allowed, and completely fine. Use cash basis unless an accountant tells you otherwise. Now you never have to worry about that sentence again.

The 20-minute monthly routine

Bookkeeping fails when it's a someday task. Make it a monthly appointment — put it on the calendar — and it stays a 20-minute chore instead of an April nightmare:

  1. Download the month's sales. Etsy and most platforms export a monthly CSV with your sales and fees in one file.
  2. Pull expenses from one account. If the shop has its own card, this is a single statement — highlight the business lines.
  3. Drop each transaction into a category. Same buckets every month. Don't overthink edge cases; be consistent, not perfect.
  4. Read your four numbers. Revenue, COGS, operating expenses, net profit. Compare to last month.
  5. Set aside tax money. Move a percentage of profit to a separate account now, so quarterly taxes are money you already have.

Why bookkeeping pays you back beyond taxes

Tax-readiness is the obvious payoff, but the bigger one is decisions. Clean books tell you which products actually earn, whether that new material supplier really is cheaper, and when your "busy" months are quietly your least profitable. A shop that keeps books makes pricing and inventory choices with eyes open; a shop that doesn't is flying by feel. This is the difference between tracking profit on purpose and hoping.

Frequently asked questions

How do I do bookkeeping for a small Etsy shop?

Record money in (sales and the fees on them) and money out (materials, shipping, software, ads) into a fixed set of categories, using a separate account for the business. Do it in a 20-minute monthly routine: download the month's sales CSV, categorize expenses from one statement, read your four numbers (revenue, COGS, expenses, net profit), and set aside tax money. Cash basis is fine for nearly all small sellers.

Do I need accounting software for an Etsy shop?

Not to start. A well-structured spreadsheet or dashboard handles sales, expenses, categories, profit, and tax prep for most solo sellers at no monthly cost. Dedicated software makes sense once you add payroll, inventory across locations, or an accountant who asks for it. The habit matters far more than the tool.

Should a small shop use cash or accrual accounting?

Cash basis for almost every small seller — you record income when you're actually paid and expenses when you actually pay them, which is simpler and allowed. Accrual (recording when earned or owed) is rarely necessary for a solo shop. Use cash basis unless an accountant specifically advises otherwise.
