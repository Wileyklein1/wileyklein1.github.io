How to Increase Average Order Value (Sell More Without More Traffic)

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Getting more traffic is hard, slow, and often expensive. Getting the buyers you already have to spend a little more is fast, free, and compounding. That lever is average order value (AOV) — the average dollar amount per order — and nudging it up is one of the highest-return moves a small shop can make, because the traffic and the sale are already paid for. Here's how to raise it without being pushy.

Why AOV is the efficient lever

Every order already cost you something to win — the fee, the ad, the time, the marketplace's cut. When you raise the value of that order, most of the extra flows closer to profit because you're not paying to acquire a new customer for it. A shop that lifts AOV from $22 to $28 on the same number of orders just gave itself a ~27% revenue increase with zero new traffic.

The math is stark: a 20% lift in average order value has the same revenue impact as a 20% increase in traffic — but it's usually far cheaper and faster to achieve, because you're working with buyers who've already decided to trust you.

The proven ways to raise AOV

  1. Bundle related products. Package items that naturally go together at a small discount versus buying separately. The buyer feels they got a deal; you booked a bigger order. This is the single most reliable AOV lever for product sellers.
  2. Offer a good-better-best tier. Three versions (basic / standard / deluxe) anchor the middle and let willing buyers spend more. Many will pick 'better' simply because it exists.
  3. Set a free-shipping or gift threshold. 'Free shipping over $35' reliably nudges a $28 cart up to $35 — the buyer adds an item to 'unlock' the perk, and you often net more than the shipping cost.
  4. Suggest a relevant add-on at checkout — the refill, the matching item, gift wrap. A small, genuinely useful add-on is welcome, not annoying.
  5. Sell the set, show the collection. Photograph and describe how items pair, so a candle becomes a candle-plus-melts order.

Do it without feeling salesy

The difference between a helpful upsell and an annoying one is relevance. Suggesting a matching item or a money-saving bundle genuinely helps a buyer who already wants what you make. Pushing unrelated stuff to pad the total erodes trust. The rule: only ever suggest something that makes the original purchase better or cheaper-per-unit. Frame it as a saving or a completion, never as pressure.

Price the bundles so they actually pay

A bundle discount only works if the bigger order still makes you money — a too-generous bundle can lift revenue while quietly shrinking profit. Before you set a bundle or threshold price, run the numbers so the discount comes out of margin you can spare, not margin you need. Frequently asked questions

What is average order value and why does it matter?

Average order value (AOV) is the average dollar amount customers spend per order. It matters because raising it increases revenue and profit without needing more traffic — the sale is already won and paid for, so most of the extra spend flows closer to profit. A 20% lift in AOV can match the revenue impact of a 20% traffic increase, usually far more cheaply.

How can a small shop increase average order value?

The most reliable levers are bundling related products at a small discount, offering good-better-best tiers that anchor a middle option, setting a free-shipping or gift threshold that nudges carts higher, and suggesting a genuinely relevant add-on at checkout. Keep every suggestion relevant to what the buyer already wants so it helps rather than annoys.

Do bundle discounts hurt profit?

They can if priced carelessly — a too-generous bundle raises revenue while shrinking profit. The fix is to price the bundle so its discount comes out of margin you can spare, not margin you need. Run the numbers on the bundled order first so the bigger sale still makes you money after the discount and any fees.
