Etsy Fees Explained (2026): What You Actually Keep Per Sale

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

"I sold it for $24" and "I kept $24" are two very different sentences on Etsy. Between the listing fee, the transaction fee, payment processing, and the fees charged on the shipping you collect, a healthy-looking sale can quietly hand back a fifth of its price. Here's every fee, what it's for, and what a real sale nets — no scare tactics, just the arithmetic.

Fees change. The rates below reflect Etsy's published 2026 US structure and are here to show you how the math works — always confirm the current numbers in your own Etsy account before you rely on them.

The four fees on (almost) every sale

FeeTypical 2026 rateWhat it's for
Listing fee$0.20 per listingCharged when you publish or auto-renew a listing, whether or not it sells.
Transaction fee6.5% of item + shippingEtsy's cut of the sale, applied to the item price and the shipping you charge.
Payment processing~3% + $0.25 (US)Card/processing fee via Etsy Payments; varies by country.
Shipping transaction fee6.5% of shipping chargedYes — the 6.5% transaction fee also applies to the shipping you collect.

There are optional fees on top: Etsy Ads (only if you opt in), Offsite Ads (a share on sales Etsy sourced from external ads — mandatory above a revenue threshold), and currency conversion. Those are situational; the four above hit nearly every order.

A worked example: the $24 sale

You sell an item for $18 and charge $6 shipping — a $24 order. Walk the fees:

LineAmount
Item price$18.00
Shipping charged$6.00
Order total$24.00
Listing fee−$0.20
Transaction fee (6.5% of $24)−$1.56
Payment processing (3% of $24 + $0.25)−$0.97
Fees total−$2.73
Net before product cost$21.27

So on a $24 order you keep about $21.27 before you've paid for the item itself or the actual postage. Fees ate roughly 11.4%. Now subtract what the item cost you to make and what shipping actually cost, and that's your profit. If the candle cost $10.50 to make and postage ran $5.20, your real profit on this order is about $5.57 — not the $24 the sale notification suggested.

Why this matters for pricing

Notice the fees are charged on shipping too. Sellers who "give free shipping" by baking postage into a higher item price still pay the 6.5% transaction fee on that portion — there is no fee-free way to move the shipping cost around. The lesson isn't "avoid fees" (you can't); it's price so your margin survives them. If you haven't set your prices from real costs yet, start with our guide to pricing handmade products.

Fees on other platforms, briefly

Etsy is not uniquely expensive — every marketplace takes a cut, just structured differently. Shopify charges you a monthly subscription plus payment processing (no per-item marketplace fee, but you supply your own traffic). Amazon Handmade takes a referral fee (commonly 15%). eBay takes a final-value fee. The only way to compare them honestly is to run the same product through each fee structure — which is exactly what the Multi-Platform Sales Tracker is built to do.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Etsy take per sale in 2026?

Plan on roughly 10–12% of the order total in core fees: a $0.20 listing fee, a 6.5% transaction fee on the item plus shipping, and about 3% + $0.25 payment processing. Optional Etsy Ads and Offsite Ads fees can add more. Always verify current rates in your Etsy account.

Does Etsy charge fees on shipping?

Yes. The 6.5% transaction fee applies to the shipping you charge the buyer, not just the item price. That's why "free shipping" baked into a higher item price is not fee-free — the transaction fee still applies to the full amount.

What's the difference between Etsy Ads and Offsite Ads?

Etsy Ads are optional in-platform promoted listings you set a daily budget for. Offsite Ads are ads Etsy runs on external sites; if a sale comes from one you pay a percentage, and participation is mandatory once your shop passes a revenue threshold. Both are separate from the core per-sale fees.
