How to Manage Inventory for a Small Business (Without Overthinking It)

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Inventory is where a lot of small businesses quietly bleed cash — not in dramatic losses, but in two slow leaks: stockouts (an order you can't fill, a supplier rush fee, a customer who moves on) and dead stock (money frozen in materials or products that aren't selling). Good inventory management is just the practice of keeping enough — and not too much — of the right things. You don't need warehouse software. You need a system and a few numbers.

What 'inventory' means for a small maker

For most solo and handmade sellers, inventory comes in two forms, and both need tracking:

  • Raw materials — the components you buy and consume making products (the wax, the beads, the blanks).
  • Finished goods — products already made and ready to sell.

Made-to-order shops live mostly in the first bucket; ready-to-ship shops carry more of the second. Either way the goal is the same: know what you have, know what it cost, and know when to reorder before it becomes a problem.

The few numbers to track per item

  1. Quantity on hand — updated as you buy, make, and sell. This is the foundation; everything else builds on it.
  2. Unit cost — what one unit of the material (or one finished item) actually cost you, which feeds directly into your true COGS and pricing.
  3. Reorder point — the stock level that triggers 'order more now,' based on how fast you use it and how long the supplier takes. (Full formula in our reorder point guide.)
  4. Supplier and lead time — who you buy it from and how long delivery takes, so a reorder is a two-minute task, not a scramble.
The two failure modes pull in opposite directions: understock and you lose sales; overstock and you freeze cash you could have used elsewhere. Inventory management is simply steering between them — and a reorder point per item is the steering wheel.

A monthly routine that keeps it honest

  1. Count the fast movers. You don't need to count everything constantly — focus on the materials and products that move quickly or matter most.
  2. Reconcile with reality. Update your on-hand numbers against an actual look at the shelf; small errors compound into stockouts if never corrected.
  3. Flag anything below its reorder point and place orders in one batch.
  4. Spot the dead stock. Anything that hasn't moved in months is frozen cash — plan a sale, a bundle, or stop reordering it.

Inventory connects to everything else

Inventory isn't a standalone chore — it's wired into your costs, your pricing, and your cash flow. Knowing material-per-unit tells you your COGS. Knowing reorder points prevents the panic buys that wreck a month's cash. And knowing what's frozen in dead stock explains why a 'profitable' shop can still feel broke — a gap our cash flow guide digs into.

Frequently asked questions

How should a small business track inventory?

Track a few numbers per item: quantity on hand (updated as you buy, make, and sell), unit cost, a reorder point, and the supplier and lead time. Do a light monthly reconcile against the actual shelf, order everything below its reorder point in one batch, and flag dead stock that hasn't moved. A maker-sized spreadsheet handles this without warehouse software.

What's the difference between raw materials and finished goods inventory?

Raw materials are the components you buy and consume while making products; finished goods are completed products ready to sell. Made-to-order shops carry mostly raw materials, while ready-to-ship shops hold more finished goods. Both need tracking so you know what you have, what it cost, and when to reorder.

How do I avoid having too much inventory?

Set a reorder point for each item based on how fast you use it and your supplier's lead time, and reorder only when you hit it — this prevents both stockouts and overbuying. Review monthly for dead stock (anything that hasn't sold in months), since that's cash frozen on your shelf, and clear it with a sale or bundle rather than reordering more.
