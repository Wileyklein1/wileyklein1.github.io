Etsy SEO: How to Find Keywords That Actually Get Your Listings Found

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read

You can have the best product and the nicest photos on the platform, and still make zero sales — because no one can find you. On a marketplace, being found is a keyword problem. Etsy SEO isn't a dark art; it's the straightforward practice of using the exact words your buyers type into search, in the places search looks. Here's how it actually works and how to do the research without guessing.

How Etsy search decides who shows up

Simplified, Etsy matches a shopper's search to listings in two stages: a relevance pass (does your title, tags, and attributes contain the words they searched?) and a ranking pass (among relevant listings, which does it show first, based on signals like recent sales, click-through, reviews, shipping, and listing quality). You control the first stage completely and influence the second.

The number-one Etsy SEO mistake is writing for yourself instead of the buyer. 'My Whimsical Woodland Collection' contains zero words anyone searches. 'Fox Nursery Print, Woodland Animal Wall Art' contains three real searches. Match the buyer's words, not your branding.

How to find the keywords buyers actually use

  1. Use Etsy's own search bar. Start typing what your product is and watch the autocomplete suggestions — those are real, popular searches straight from the source.
  2. Read the 'related searches' at the top of results pages and the tags on the top-ranking listings in your niche (their success is a hint about what works).
  3. Think in buyer language, not maker language. Buyers search by occasion, recipient, style, color, and use ('personalized dog mom gift'), not by your internal product names.
  4. Favor specific long-tail phrases. 'Gift' is impossibly competitive; 'teacher appreciation gift under 20' is winnable and converts better because the searcher knows exactly what they want.

Where to put the keywords

  • Title: front-load your strongest phrase, then stack specific descriptors — material, style, use, recipient. Write for a human, but make sure the searchable phrase is unmistakably present.
  • Tags: use every slot, each a distinct multi-word phrase (not single words), covering synonyms and use-cases. Don't waste tags repeating a word already in another tag.
  • Attributes and categories: fill them all — Etsy uses them for filtering and relevance, and empty ones are missed matches.
  • First line of the description: restate what it is and who it's for in searchable words; some surfaces and previews pull from it.

SEO gets you found — the rest gets you the sale

Keywords put you in the search results. Whether the shopper then clicks and buys depends on your thumbnail, price, and reviews — which is why SEO can't be separated from the rest of a good listing. Frequently asked questions

How does Etsy SEO work?

Etsy search first checks relevance — whether your title, tags, attributes, and categories contain the words a shopper searched — then ranks the relevant listings using signals like recent sales, click-through rate, reviews, and listing quality. You fully control relevance by using the buyer's exact search words, and you influence ranking through good photos, pricing, and service that drive clicks and sales.

How do I find good keywords for my Etsy listings?

Start with Etsy's own search autocomplete — those suggestions are real popular searches. Check related searches and the tags on top-ranking listings in your niche, think in buyer language (occasion, recipient, style, use) rather than your product names, and favor specific long-tail phrases like 'teacher appreciation gift under 20' over broad, hyper-competitive words like 'gift.'

Should I use single words or phrases in Etsy tags?

Use multi-word phrases. Each tag slot should be a distinct phrase a buyer might actually type, covering synonyms and use-cases — not single words or repeats of a word already used elsewhere. Filling every tag slot with real, varied search phrases gives your listing the most chances to match a query.
