Standard Operating Procedures for a Handmade Shop (Why You Need Them, With Examples)

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

"SOP" sounds like corporate overkill for a shop run from a spare bedroom. It isn't. A standard operating procedure is just the written-down version of how you do a recurring task — and for a solo seller, that written version is the difference between a shop that runs smoothly and one that lives entirely in your head, breaking the moment you're sick, busy, or finally trying to hand off a task. Here's why they matter and which ones to write first.

Why a one-person shop needs SOPs

  • Consistency. When fulfillment follows the same steps every time, quality stops depending on your mood or memory. Buyers get the same good experience on order 200 as on order 2.
  • Speed. A checklist you follow is faster than re-deciding the steps each time. You stop leaving out the thank-you card or forgetting to update inventory.
  • Fewer mistakes. Most shop errors — wrong item, missed message, forgotten restock — are process gaps, not skill gaps. An SOP closes the gap.
  • You can finally delegate. The first time you hire help or ask a partner to cover, an SOP is what lets them do the task correctly without you hovering.
  • Peace of mind. A shop that runs on documented processes can survive a week where you're not at your best.
An SOP doesn't have to be formal. A numbered list titled 'How I pack and ship an order' pinned above your workbench is a standard operating procedure. The value is in writing it down once, not in making it fancy.

The core SOPs every seller should have

You don't need fifty. Start with the handful of tasks you do most and can least afford to do wrong:

  1. Order fulfillment — from 'new order' to 'shipped': verify, make/pick, quality-check, pack, label, ship, mark fulfilled, update inventory.
  2. Restock / reorder — when and how you reorder materials, from whom, and how you record the cost (this ties directly into knowing your true COGS).
  3. Customer service — how fast you reply, your tone, and your standard responses to the messages you get again and again (shipping time, customization, 'where's my order').
  4. Returns & problems — your policy and the exact steps for a damaged item, a wrong address, or a lost package, so a stressful moment follows a calm script.
  5. Listing / launch — the repeatable steps to publish a new product well (see our launch checklist).

How to write one in ten minutes

  1. Pick a task you did today.
  2. Write down every step you took, in order, as you remember them.
  3. Do the task once more and correct the list where reality differed.
  4. Note the 'gotchas' — the easy-to-forget step, the thing that goes wrong.
  5. Save it where you'll actually see it when doing the task.

That's it. The hardest SOP is the first one; after that it's a habit. Review each one every few months and update it when your process changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is a standard operating procedure (SOP)?

An SOP is a written, step-by-step description of how you do a recurring task — like packing an order or handling a return. It turns a process that lives in your head into a repeatable checklist, so the task gets done the same correct way every time, even when you're busy or someone else covers for you.

Does a one-person business really need SOPs?

Yes. For a solo seller, SOPs deliver consistency, speed, and fewer mistakes, and they make it possible to delegate or take time off without the shop breaking. They don't need to be formal — a numbered 'how I ship an order' list counts. The value is writing it down once.

Which SOPs should a handmade shop write first?

Start with the tasks you do most and can least afford to get wrong: order fulfillment, restock/reorder, customer service responses, and returns or problem handling. Add a listing/launch SOP so new products go live consistently. A handful of solid SOPs covers most of a small shop's operations.
