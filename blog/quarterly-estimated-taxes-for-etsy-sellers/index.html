Quarterly Estimated Taxes for Etsy Sellers: When to Pay and How Much to Set Aside

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read
This is a plain-English overview to help you get organized — it is not tax advice. Thresholds, dates, and safe-harbor rules change and depend on your situation. Always confirm current figures with the IRS and a qualified tax professional.

When you have a normal job, an employer quietly withholds taxes from every paycheck, so April is mostly a formality. When you sell on Etsy as your own boss, nobody does that withholding for you — which means the IRS generally expects you to pay tax throughout the year yourself, in four installments called estimated taxes. Skip them and you can owe a penalty even if you pay in full in April. Here's how to tell if you owe them, how much to set aside, and how to make it painless.

Do you even owe quarterly taxes?

Not every seller does. As a general rule, the IRS expects quarterly estimated payments if you'll owe roughly $1,000 or more in tax for the year from income that isn't withheld. A few practical cases:

  • Etsy is a small side hobby earning little profit — you may owe little or nothing extra, especially if a day-job withholds enough to cover it.
  • Etsy is a meaningful side income — you likely cross the threshold and should pay quarterly, or increase withholding at a day job to compensate.
  • Etsy is your main income — you almost certainly owe quarterly estimates, including self-employment tax.
Don't forget self-employment tax. Beyond income tax, profit from self-employment carries an additional ~15.3% for Social Security and Medicare — the portion an employer would normally split with you. It's why setting aside "just a little for taxes" often falls short. Plan for income tax and SE tax together.

The four payment dates

Estimated taxes are due four times a year, each covering a slice of the year's income. The dates are roughly consistent year to year (they shift when one lands on a weekend or holiday):

PaymentCovers income fromTypical due date
Q1January – MarchMid-April
Q2April – MayMid-June
Q3June – AugustMid-September
Q4September – DecemberMid-January (next year)

Note the quarters aren't even three-month blocks — Q2 covers two months, Q4 covers four. Always confirm the exact dates for the current year on the IRS site, and pay online through IRS Direct Pay or EFTPS.

How much to set aside

The simplest sustainable habit is to set aside a percentage of every profitable sale into a separate account the moment the money lands — so the quarterly payment is money you already have, not a scramble. A common starting range for solo sellers is 25–30% of profit (covering both income and self-employment tax), but your right number depends on your bracket and other income.

Two ways to size your payments without guessing:

  1. Percentage-of-profit set-aside — park 25–30% of each sale's profit as you go; pay a quarter of your running set-aside each due date. Simple and self-correcting as income rises and falls.
  2. Safe harbor — pay in at least as much as last year's total tax (a higher percentage for higher earners), split into four. Meet the safe harbor and you generally avoid an underpayment penalty even if you owe more at filing. Confirm the current safe-harbor percentages.

The penalty you're avoiding

If you owe enough and don't pay throughout the year, the IRS can charge an underpayment penalty — effectively interest on the tax you should have paid earlier. It's usually not huge, but it's avoidable money, and it stings precisely because it's for a paperwork miss rather than the tax itself. Paying reasonable quarterly estimates (or hitting the safe harbor) is how you sidestep it.

Frequently asked questions

Do Etsy sellers have to pay quarterly taxes?

Generally, if you'll owe about $1,000 or more in tax for the year on income that isn't withheld, the IRS expects quarterly estimated payments. A small hobby with little profit may not trigger it, especially if a day job withholds enough; meaningful or full-time Etsy income usually does. Confirm your situation with a tax professional — this is organizational guidance, not tax advice.

How much should I set aside for quarterly taxes?

Many solo sellers set aside 25–30% of profit to cover both income tax and self-employment tax (~15.3% on its own), moved to a separate account as sales come in. Your exact rate depends on your bracket and other income. Alternatively, use the safe-harbor method — paying in at least last year's tax, split four ways — and confirm current figures with a professional.

When are quarterly estimated taxes due?

There are four payments a year, typically due in mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, and mid-January of the following year. The quarters aren't even three-month blocks, and dates shift when they fall on a weekend or holiday, so confirm the exact current-year dates on the IRS website and pay via IRS Direct Pay or EFTPS.
