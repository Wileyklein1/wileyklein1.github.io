Etsy vs Shopify: Which Is Actually More Profitable for a Small Seller?

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read

"Should I sell on Etsy or Shopify?" is really two questions wearing one coat. One is about fees — which platform takes less per sale. The other is about traffic — which platform actually brings you buyers. The honest answer is that they win on opposite ones, and the right choice depends on a number only you can supply. Let's make it concrete.

The core trade-off in one line: Etsy charges more per sale but hands you buyers; Shopify charges less per sale but you must find the buyers yourself. Neither is "cheaper" in the abstract — it depends entirely on where your traffic comes from.

Where Etsy wins

  • You have no audience yet. Etsy's built-in search and shopper base can put you in front of buyers on day one. That discovery is what the higher fees are buying.
  • Low or irregular volume. No monthly subscription means Etsy costs nothing when you're not selling — friendly to a seasonal or side operation.
  • You'd rather not run marketing. The marketplace does a chunk of the demand generation for you.

Where Shopify wins

  • You have your own traffic. An email list, a social following, or repeat customers you can send straight to your store — no marketplace cut needed.
  • Higher volume. Past a certain monthly sales level, the flat subscription is cheaper per order than Etsy's percentage, and the gap widens as you grow.
  • You want the brand and the customer relationship. Your store, your data, your email list — assets Etsy keeps for itself.

The math that actually decides it

Plug a real month into both. Say you sell 60 orders at a $22 average — $1,320 in revenue:

EtsyShopify (Basic-tier example)
Revenue$1,320$1,320
Subscription$0~$39
Per-sale fees~$139 (transaction + processing)~$56 (processing only)
Total platform cost~$139~$95
But traffic came from…Etsy, for freeYou had to drive it

On raw fees Shopify looks cheaper here. But the last row is the whole ballgame: if those 60 orders happened because Etsy shoppers found you, Etsy's extra ~$44 bought $1,320 of sales you might not have made otherwise — a bargain. If you'd have gotten those buyers to a Shopify store anyway through your own audience, Shopify keeps you more money. Many established sellers end up running both: Etsy for discovery, Shopify for repeat and brand sales.

Frequently asked questions

Is Etsy or Shopify cheaper?

It depends on volume and where your traffic comes from. Etsy has no monthly fee but takes a larger cut per sale (~6.5% transaction plus processing). Shopify charges a monthly subscription but lower per-sale fees. At higher volume Shopify's flat cost often wins on fees — but only if you can supply your own buyers, which Etsy provides built-in.

Can I sell on both Etsy and Shopify?

Yes, and many established sellers do: Etsy for discovery and new-customer traffic, Shopify for repeat buyers and brand-owned sales. Tracking both in one place is the only way to see your true combined profit and which channel actually pays you best.

Which platform is better for a brand-new seller?

For most sellers with no existing audience, Etsy's built-in shopper traffic makes it the easier place to make first sales — its higher fees are effectively paying for discovery. Once you've built an email list or following, adding or moving to Shopify can keep more of each sale.
