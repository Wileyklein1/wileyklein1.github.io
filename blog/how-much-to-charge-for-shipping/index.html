How Much Should You Charge for Shipping? (Free Shipping vs Flat Rate, Done Right)

By The LedgerLane Team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read

Shipping is where a lot of small sellers quietly lose money without ever noticing. The item price gets all the attention; shipping gets a guess. Then a heavier-than-expected package, a fee charged on the shipping itself, and a "free shipping" promise made in a hopeful moment combine into an order that costs you money to fulfill. Shipping isn't a rounding error — on a $20 product it's often the single biggest line after the item cost. Here's how to charge for it on purpose.

First, know what a shipment actually costs you

Before you can charge for shipping, you need its true cost — and it's more than the postage label:

  • Postage — the actual carrier rate for the weight, size, and destination. This varies more than sellers expect; a package to the next state and one across the country can differ by dollars.
  • Packaging — box or mailer, tape, filler, tissue, the thank-you card. A padded mailer plus filler can run $0.50–$1.50 before the label.
  • The fee on shipping — on most marketplaces the transaction fee applies to the shipping you charge, not just the item. Etsy's 6.5%, for example, is charged on the shipping line too.
  • Your time — packing takes minutes per order. It's real labor even if you don't put it on the invoice.
The most expensive shipping mistake is quoting a flat rate from memory ("shipping's like five bucks") and never checking it against a real label. Weigh a packed parcel once and price the carrier's actual rate — guesses always drift low, and the gap comes straight out of your margin.

The three ways to charge — and when each wins

MethodHow it worksBest when
Calculated / live ratesBuyer pays the carrier's exact rate by weight and destinationHeavy, bulky, or wildly varying items — you never over- or under-charge
Flat rateOne shipping price for everything (or per category)Uniform, lightweight products where costs barely vary
Free shippingShipping cost is built into a higher item priceSmall, light items where you can absorb it — and buyers filter for it

None of these is "the right one." A candle maker shipping uniform 12-oz jars can flat-rate safely. A seller with items from a keychain to a framed print needs calculated rates or they'll lose money on the heavy end and overcharge on the light end (which costs sales).

The truth about "free shipping"

Free shipping is never free — it's shipping cost moved into the item price. That's not a trick; done right it's smart, because many buyers filter and sort for free shipping and some platforms reward it in search. But it only works if you actually raise the item price to cover the postage. The failure mode is offering free shipping and keeping the old price, turning your margin into the shipping budget.

And remember the fee: if you bake $6 of postage into the item price, the marketplace transaction fee now applies to that $6 too. There is no fee-free way to move shipping around — a reality we cover in our Etsy fees breakdown. So when you fold shipping into the price, nudge it up a little further to cover the extra fee, not just the raw postage.

A worked example

You sell an $18 item that costs $5.20 to actually ship (postage + a $0.80 mailer). Compare charging $6 shipping vs. offering "free":

Charge $6 shipping"Free" shipping ($24 item)
Buyer pays$18 + $6 = $24$24
Real shipping cost−$5.20−$5.20
Marketplace fee (~9.5% of $24)−$2.28−$2.28
Item cost to make−$7.00−$7.00
Net profit$9.52$9.52

Notice they land in the same place — because the money is the same money, just labeled differently. The buyer often prefers the "free" version even though the total is identical. That's the real lever: same economics, better conversion. What you must never do is offer the free version at the original $18 total — that's the $5.20 shipping coming out of your $9.52, cutting your profit nearly in half.

Practical rules that keep shipping profitable

  1. Weigh a real, packed parcel for each product and price from the carrier's actual rate — not memory.
  2. Include packaging and the fee-on-shipping in the cost, not just postage.
  3. If you offer free shipping, raise the item price to cover it — postage plus the extra fee.
  4. Set shipping profiles per product type so a heavy item never ships on a light item's rate.
  5. Re-check when carrier rates change (usually annually). Frozen shipping prices silently erode margin as postage rises.

Frequently asked questions

Should I offer free shipping or charge for it?

Either can work — the key is that free shipping isn't free. If you offer it, raise the item price to cover postage, packaging, and the marketplace fee charged on shipping. Many buyers filter and sort for free shipping and some platforms favor it in search, so the same economics often converts better labeled as 'free.' Just never offer it without raising the price to cover the cost.

How do I figure out the real cost of shipping an order?

Add four things: the carrier's actual postage for a packed parcel's weight and destination, your packaging (mailer or box, tape, filler), the marketplace transaction fee charged on the shipping amount, and a bit for your packing time. Weigh a real packed parcel rather than guessing — estimates almost always run low.

Do marketplaces charge fees on shipping?

On most marketplaces, yes. Etsy's transaction fee, for example, applies to the shipping you charge as well as the item price. That's why baking shipping into a 'free shipping' item price isn't fee-free — the fee still applies to the full amount, so cover a little extra when you fold postage into the price.
